Network quality

QualityDeviceLatencyJitterLoss ChecksBasis
Measured with ICMP echo over the selected window, using the device and window chosen in Latency history above. Latency is the mean round-trip time of replies received. Jitter is the mean absolute difference between the average round-trip times of consecutive checks that both got replies (not RFC 3550 RTP jitter, and not one-way delay). Loss is probes without a reply as a share of probes sent. Time with no checks is a gap, never counted as 0 ms or as loss. A grade is the worst of the three figures against thresholds set in the inventory: application-level heuristics, not a QoS standard. This shows network performance; it does not verify or enforce QoS (DSCP marking, shaping or queueing).

Wi-Fi signal (this computer)

MeasurementValueSource
Local only: this reads the Wi-Fi adapter of the computer running NetMonLT. It never scans for nearby networks, never records other networks' names and never connects to anything. Signal quality is the percentage Windows reports (a vendor-defined figure, not dBm). RSSI in dBm is shown only when the operating system itself reports one, and is never calculated from the percentage. Link rates are the negotiated rates, not measured throughput. A dash means unavailable, not zero.
